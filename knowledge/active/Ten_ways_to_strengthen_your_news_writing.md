# Ten Ways to Strengthen Your News Writing

1. **Look for a human element** and tell the story through their eyes; a person or group of people who are impacted. Pull the reader in immediately with a poignant quote and/or a vivid description.

2. **Avoid acronyms!** If they must be used, spell out what they mean in the first reference. Avoid jargon like legal lingo as well.

3. **Create a "mission statement"** before writing: what is your story and what are you trying to accomplish by sharing it? Make the audience care!

4. **Evoke an emotion:** your audience should feel *something* when they read your work — anger, fear, joy or simply pleased that they learned something they did not know!

5. **Read your work out loud.** If you run out of breath during a lengthy sentence, consider breaking your narrative into two shorter, more powerful lines.

6. **Eliminate any words that do not advance the story.** Often, less is more. Declutter!

7. **Use only compelling quotes** and leverage overheard dialogue which can be just as relevant. Limit use of officials giving statistics. With official and expert quotes, be specific with attribution (A police official from which location? An expert from what industry? Provide names and titles.)

8. **Transport your audience to the scene.** Remember, you have five senses: sight, hearing, smell, touch, taste. Aim to include at least two per story.

9. **Don't reference someone's age or clothing, unless it is relevant to your reporting.** (i.e., If a business owner is "just 17 years old," or a mechanic has "grease-stained fingers.")

10. **Look for content others might miss.** Example: countless journalists interviewed the mother of a missing girl who had disappeared years before. Only one noticed there was tape on the light switch by the front door and asked why. Her response: "I will keep the light on until she comes home."
