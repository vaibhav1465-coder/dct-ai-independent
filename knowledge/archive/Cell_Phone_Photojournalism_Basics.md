# Cell Phone Photojournalism Essentials

## Stills

- Shoot horizontal for most news purposes. Vertical if you know it's for social/mobile.
- Capture people and emotion — NOT buildings — whenever possible.
- Shoot three basic pictures: wide (scene), medium (action), tight (detail/emotion), especially when you get to a spot news story.
- Try to tell a story with a single image. What can the audience learn from your photo?
- Check the background! Make sure there are no plants, branches or poles sticking out of a person's head.
- Aim for natural lighting. Face your subject toward a light source. Do NOT shoot someone standing in front of a window where sunlight is streaming through. They will end up as a silhouette!
- Shoot at eye level of your subject (crouch for anyone in a wheelchair or chair, as well as children). Hold the camera high to get crowd shots.
- Lead room: leave space in front of a moving subject.
- Create your own "tripod" to steady your shots: find a wall, top of a car or create an "instant tripod" with a lanyard.
- When shooting a portrait, put the subject off centre. Rule of thirds.

## Video

- Hold EVERY shot for 10 seconds at minimum before moving to the next location — please no whip pans or frequent zooming in or out.
- Shoot sequences from the same angle: wide, medium, tight.
- If shooting an interview, get cutaways, including a reverse angle or reaction shot to edit your way out of any situation.
- Try to capture clean ambient sound around you and if possible, step away from people who are talking.
- With action, let the subject move into and out of frame, rather than chasing them with the camera.

## Editing

- Crop to strengthen composition — tighten, straighten horizons, remove distractions.
- Adjust exposure/contrast lightly for clarity — never alter facts, including adding or removing objects or people.
- Keep colour natural; avoid heavy filters on news images.
- Caption every image: who, what, where, when, and why it matters.
- Prioritise the strongest 3–5 shots; trim silence/dead air; keep sequences in logical order.

## Ethics

- NEVER stage your shots or rearrange items at a crime scene, for example.
- Ask permission from an adult to use the faces of anyone under the age of 18.
- Before you hit send, ask yourself if your images reflect the truth at your location.
