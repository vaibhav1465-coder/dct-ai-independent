# Indian Express Style Guide

> **Note: This document is incomplete.**

Most importantly, **everyone in the newsroom must follow the same rules.** Consistency is critical to build audience trust and loyalty.

---

- Confirm British vs. American spelling ("colour" vs. "color")
- Consistency in reference to geographic locations (Mumbai vs. Bombay)

## Abbreviations / Acronyms

Spell out in first reference with abbreviation in parentheses; acronym can be used in future references.

## Active Voice vs. Passive Voice

Use active voice with strong verbs. Avoid passive constructions.

- Passive (avoid): "The suspect was arrested"
- Active (use): "Police arrested the suspect…"

## Age References

Always use numerals (a 5-year-old boy, not a five-year-old boy).

If age is used after a name, it is separated by commas and never parentheses.

## Opening Paragraph

Keep the lede short — ideally one sentence (20–25 words maximum).

- Focus on ONE main idea and not multiple facts crammed together.
- If a sentence has multiple commas or clauses, break it up into shorter sentences.

## Lede

Do not "back into" a story with yesterday's news or set-up. Start with what's new and present it in the most compelling way possible. The context can come in the second paragraph.

## Photo Captions

Must be labelled properly, especially to distinguish between suspects and victims of crimes.
